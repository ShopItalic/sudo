Recording and everyday controls

FeatureBehavior in this firmware
Hold to recordHolding starts push-to-talk; releasing stops capture and finalizes the file. Works while connected and standalone. The default limit is ten seconds; it is configurable, including no duration limit.
Optional hands-free recordingDouble tap starts a memo and another double tap stops it. A hold also stops a gesture-owned memo, consuming that contact until release. Double tap is disabled on fresh settings; existing saved choices persist. Default memo limit is zero, meaning no configured duration cap, subject to storage, battery and faults.
App Start and StopThe versioned protocol uses recording identities and explicit results. Retries converge on the same recording. Stop success waits for finalization; state queries resolve lost responses. Legacy app recording controls are bridged through the same owner.
Audio formatMono IMA ADPCM, nominally 8 kHz and approximately 32 kbps. Each 220-byte encoded block represents 440 PCM samples. Capture preserves the supplier's every-other-sample decimation and continuous encoder state. Opus and true 16 kHz capture are not enabled.
Local backupA file is opened before successful capture start. Each accepted encoded block is written locally before optional live delivery. Phone disconnect, app absence or failed preview does not take ownership of the microphone or delete the recording.
Capture safeguardsEight bounded DMA slots, session identities, ordered blocks and explicit overflow errors. Complete queued blocks drain before finalization. A stale or invalid touch report stops PTT; the touch lease is 750 ms. A stalled PDM peripheral and stop-drain timeout yield partial/error outcomes rather than invented success.

Storage, phone connection and transfer

FeatureBehavior in this firmware
Durable recording metadataFull 64-bit recording identity, export filename, trigger, duration limit, bytes, frames, CRC, completion and recovery state. Audio and checksummed attributes live in LittleFS. Checkpoints target every 1,000 ms or 4,096 accepted bytes; this is not a guarantee of physical flash completion time.
Recovery after interruptionCold mounts expose a verified durable prefix as partial/recovered. Recovery does not restart the microphone or format the flash. Uncheckpointed audio can be lost after power failure. Empty clips and write/sync/close failures are explicit.
Preserve unsynced audioFull storage rejects or terminates a new recording with an error. It does not automatically overwrite the oldest pending file. Native deletion requires an exact nonzero byte count and CRC plus a persisted, checked phone-custody receipt.
Catalog and resumeThe phone can enumerate metadata, verify a stored file, download it, acknowledge progress, cancel and resume. Verification scans use bounded chunks. A resumed suffix alone is insufficient phone-side proof of the whole recording.
Reliable BLE schedulingOne queued data writer, connection epochs, bounded send retries, checksummed fragments and transfer tokens reject stale work. Native transfer sends up to four fragments of one message per worker pass. Delayed cumulative ACKs remain valid during retransmission.
Abandoned native transfersThirty seconds without real verification or advancing ACK progress cancels the attempt and permits idle flash shutdown. Duplicate ACKs and same-token retries do not extend it. A higher token permits a fresh attempt; recording data and custody remain intact.
Live phone previewA bounded live prefix, READY lease and stream acknowledgments support same-iPhone dictation through the matching app. Missing frames, stale connections or expired leases disable preview while keeping the local file for later sync. Speech recognition and keyboard insertion execute on the phone.
Matching SDK optimizationThe phone may reuse an exact full-download SHA/CRC proof on the same connection to avoid a second complete Bluetooth read during cleanup. Reconnects, partial resumes or changed receipts retain the conservative verification path. This feature requires the matching SDK; it is not implemented by firmware alone.
Factory-file compatibilityExisting factory root recordings remain readable through the legacy filename bridge. Legacy list, read, resume and space queries are retained. Legacy delete, format and one-click batch operations are rejected; safe custody migration for old files remains unfinished.

Feedback, settings and power

FeatureBehavior in this firmware
Recording lightGreen while recording when lights are enabled. Recording takes priority over connection and custom-light events. The desired state survives event-queue contention.
Recording hapticsDefault active pulses: 120 ms at start, 280 ms after a successful local save, and 400 ms for error feedback. The normal stop cue confirms local storage, not successful transcription or insertion.
Phone insertion confirmationTwo 80 ms pulses require the matching phone to report insertion of this exact dictation. Firmware checks recording identity, live token, connection and a ten-second terminal window. Duplicates do not replay the cue. New capture, link change or mute cancels pending confirmation. The keyboard receipt acknowledges insertion, not the host app's durable save.
Persisted customizationApplication lights and haptics can be disabled independently. Haptic strength is 1–100% of the retained PWM range; start/stop pulse durations are 20–400 ms in 20 ms increments. Settings change while idle and are acknowledged only after persisted readback. Bootloader and pre-settings startup output are outside the application mute boundary.
Touch tuningDefault set/clear thresholds: 54/52. Supported set range: 32–80; clear: 30 through set−2. Desired values persist; application to the sensor waits for a no-contact communication window and requires register readback. Pending, applied and I/O-error states remain distinct.
Battery reportingChecked ADC acquisition, serialized sampling and a trimmed filter avoid treating failed reads as zero percent. Charging-phase changes reset filter history. Valid readings can recover after a transient low sample.
Motor-aware samplingBattery reads skip motor activity and a provisional 250 ms settling interval; activity during acquisition invalidates the batch. Standard-board battery ADC acquisition is 40 μs. The supplier voltage curve and PMIC protections are retained and still require calibration on actual cells.
Idle peripheral powerMicrophone power is released after capture cleanup. Flash powers down after 500 ms idle when recording and archive work are inactive. Actual current consumption and battery life have not been measured.

Retained platform support and exclusions

Bluetooth advertising, pairing/bonding, device/version information, RTC/time support, watchdog, power/charging support and the existing DFU service remain in the selected platform. Motion and temperature support are retained; they are not new qualified health features. The HID service identity remains for compatibility, while optional swipe, media, camera and presentation actions are disabled by the Sudo profile. PPG health capture is disabled. Wi-Fi, on-Ring speech recognition, arbitrary UTF-8-to-HID typing and Mac forwarding are not implemented features of this release.

The existing DFU service does not make the downloaded S03 ZIP an OTA update: S03 RC1 is an unsigned engineering application. Supplier signing, update compatibility and demonstrated recovery remain required.

Thoughtful simplification opportunities

These are recommendations, not firmware changes made by this audit. Preserve the published S03 candidate while qualifying a separate cleanup change.

PriorityCandidate and boundary
First: truthful task-start checksNine inherited task-start sites compare the integer xTaskCreate result against NULL. The bundled FreeRTOS returns -1 on allocation failure, which passes that test and can log success. Compare with pdPASS and explicitly handle a required task failing to start. The new Sudo worker already checks pdPASS. Verify this with allocation-failure injection; do not suppress the compiler warning.
First: dead live statebc_voice_service.live has no member reads or writes. live_pending is only initialized/cleared to false, never set true. The actual implementation uses live_prefix and tx. Remove the unused message and redundant conditions, adjust the one structural test assertion, then rerun service/worker tests and the ARM build. Measure actual struct/RAM savings rather than assume them.
First: current documentationMake S03 the current overview and label S01/S02 evidence historical. This audit corrects three stale overview references. Keep the original supplier change history and immutable release checksums.
Next: supplier compatibility stubsThe worker ends with health-file no-ops, old online/offline recording hooks and legacy upload wrappers. Some still have callers; several already disappear through linker garbage collection. Remove obsolete callers and stubs together only in the Sudo target. This mainly reduces maintenance burden; do not promise a battery or binary-size benefit without comparing outputs.
Next: narrow active command handlingThe large supplier dispatcher contains unrelated health, factory, Wi-Fi and optional control branches even though the voice profile disables or intercepts many of them. A small explicit Sudo dispatch surface could reduce reachability and auditing work. First enumerate supported phone commands and preserve time, identity, pairing, battery, motion requirements and update compatibility.
Later: legacy archive retirementThe legacy archive adapter remains responsible for existing factory recordings and older filename-based reads. Delete it only after the supported app fleet and all old recordings have a tested native migration. Removing it now would lose useful compatibility.
Keep the reliability mechanismsLocal backup, durable receipt/tombstone ordering, CRC checks, session/token guards, stop/drain ownership and bounded retries protect against different failures. They should not be merged or removed merely to reduce line count. Preserve supplier sources/licenses, including excluded Opus sources, for provenance and future evaluation.

What still needs a physical test

  1. Boot and sustained mixed use on an identified spare standard board; measure actual task/interrupt stacks, minimum free heap and stop latency.
  2. Microphone sample timing, real supplier ADPCM output, beginning/end audio, clipping, intelligibility and phone decoding.
  3. BLE goodput and reconnect behavior with a real iPhone, including keyboard insertion and app suspension.
  4. Touch reliability, motor/LED timing, battery voltage/percentage, charging and total current draw.
  5. Physical power cuts and long-lived storage with many recordings and retained receipt tombstones. Catalog and filename-collision scans depend on entry count; this pass does not establish lifetime capacity or worst-case scan latency.
  6. Supplier compiler reproduction and binary-library ABI review. Ten pointer/integer comparison compiler warnings, two supplier wchar warnings and eight libnosys warnings remain. Nine comparison warnings are the task-start checks described above; the other is in the unused gamepad-report source. GNU stdout is unavailable through libnosys, so it is not a usable diagnostic transport as built.
  7. Signed update packaging and demonstrated interrupted-update recovery. The original factory distribution is not a backup of an individual Ring.
